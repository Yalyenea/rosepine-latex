\begin{postercolumn}
\postercolumnheading{01}{Problem}

\begin{posterblock}{Background}
  In high-dimensional covariance estimation the dimension $p$ and the sample
  size $n$ are often of the same order. The classical limit with $p$ fixed
  and $n\to\infty$ fails: sample eigenvalues \textbf{systematically} spread
  into a continuous bulk.

  The same bulk appears in PCA, spectral clustering, cleaning of financial
  correlation matrices, and array signal processing. Draw the noise window
  first, then talk about spikes---not a scree plot alone.
\end{posterblock}

\posterblockgap

\begin{posterblock}{Question}
  Columns of $X\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}$ are i.i.d.\ mean-zero with covariance
  $\Sigma$, and $S_n=n^{-1}XX^\top$. When $p,n\to\infty$ and
  $\gamma=p/n\to\gamma_\star\in(0,1]$, what is the limit of
  \[
    \mu_n=\frac1p\sum_{i=1}^p \delta_{\lambda_i(S_n)}\,?
  \]
  How does it depend on $\mu_\Sigma$, and how large is the finite-$(p,n)$ bias?
  Looking only at the largest eigenvalue easily mistakes the right bulk edge
  for a signal.
\end{posterblock}

\posterblockgap

\begin{posterblock}[posterquiet]{Notation}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\lambda_\pm=(1\pm\sqrt{\gamma})^2$: edges of the noise bulk
    \item $m(z)$: Stieltjes transform, $\operatorname{Im}z>0$
    \item $\mu_{\mathrm{MP},\gamma}$: Marchenko--Pastur law of ratio $\gamma$
    \item spikes: finitely many outliers of $\Sigma$
    \item $\ell_c=1+\sqrt{\gamma}$: BBP critical value
    \item $\gamma=p/n$: aspect ratio that sets the window width
  \end{itemize}
  White noise $\Sigma=I_p$ is the reference; coloured populations enter through
  Silverstein. Every later threshold starts from these symbols.
\end{posterblock}
\end{postercolumn}

\begin{postercolumn}
\postercolumnheading{02}{Spectrum}

\begin{posterblock}{Marchenko--Pastur}
  \textbf{Theorem (white noise).}
  If $\Sigma=I_p$ and entries have four finite moments, $\mu_n$ converges
  weakly almost surely to
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathrm{MP},\gamma}(\lambda)
      &=\frac{\sqrt{(\lambda_+-\lambda)(\lambda-\lambda_-)}}{2\pi\gamma\lambda} \\
      &\quad{}\times\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.
    \end{aligned}
  \]
  The Stieltjes transform solves
  $\gamma z m^2+(z+\gamma-1)m+1=0$ ($\operatorname{Im}m>0$).
  Even if every population eigenvalue equals $1$, the sample spectrum fills
  $[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$; for $\gamma=1/4$ the window is about $[0.25,\,2.25]$.
\end{posterblock}

\posterblockgap

\begin{posterblock}{Spikes and BBP}
  A general $\Sigma$ is encoded by Silverstein's equation
  \[
    m=m_{\mu_\Sigma}\!\Bigl(z-\gamma\int\frac{t}{1+tm}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_\Sigma(t)\Bigr).
  \]
  An isolated eigenvalue $\ell>\ell_c=1+\sqrt{\gamma}$ undergoes the
  \textbf{BBP transition}: below threshold it is swallowed by the bulk; above,
  $\lambda_{\mathrm{spike}}\approx \ell+\gamma\ell/(\ell-1)$,
  with overlap $1-\gamma/(\ell-1)^2$.
  That is the smallest SNR PCA can see.
\end{posterblock}

\begin{postercolumn}
\postercolumnheading{03}{Practice}

\begin{posterblock}{Practical steps}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Estimate $\hat\gamma$ and the noise level; draw $[\hat\lambda_-,\hat\lambda_+]$.
    \item Treat in-bulk eigenvalues as noise; keep only outliers as signal.
    \item For a covariance estimate, use Ledoit--Peche nonlinear shrinkage.
    \item Report edges, spike locations, and overlaps together.
    \item Repeat tens of times; bulk mass should sit near $1$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{posterblock}

\posterblockgap

\begin{posterblock}[posterfeature]{Takeaways}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A high-dimensional sample spectrum is a noise bulk first, spikes second.
    \item MP edges give a usable signal/noise cut.
    \item The BBP threshold marks what cannot be seen---do not hunt inside the window.
    \item Do not force the white-noise formula on a coloured $\Sigma$.
    \item Report edges, spike locations, and overlaps, not only a scree plot.
    \item This sheet is a filled A0 landscape demo. Swap in your content.
  \end{itemize}
\end{posterblock}
